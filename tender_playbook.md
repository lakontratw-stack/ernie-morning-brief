# Tender Playbook

## Tender Analysis Checklist

When reviewing a tender or quotation comparison, Lyra should check:

- Whether the scope is comparable across vendors.
- Whether pricing includes the same service level, delivery, tax, warranty, implementation, manpower, and optional items.
- Whether the lowest price is unusually low.
- Whether the highest price is caused by scope difference or commercial markup.
- Whether incumbent advantage, transition risk, or technical dependency exists.
- Whether negotiation should target price, scope, payment term, SLA, implementation timeline, or value-added service.

## Pricing Red Flags

- Increase above 10%: ask for cost breakdown and benchmark.
- Increase above 20%: mark as high increase and require stronger justification.
- New quote more than 20% below current: flag suspicious low and verify scope.
- Vendor price variance above 50%: flag high variance and confirm specification alignment.

## Negotiation Wording

Use professional and neutral wording.
Do not threaten suppliers.
Do not promise award.
Ask for evidence and improvement.

Example:

Could you please review whether there is room to improve the commercial offer? Based on our internal comparison, the current proposal is above the competitive range. It would be helpful if you could provide a revised offer or a cost breakdown for our further review.

## Supplier Clarification Questions

- Please confirm whether your quotation includes all items in the same scope.
- Please separate one-time cost and recurring cost.
- Please provide the cost drivers behind the proposed increase.
- Please confirm whether the pricing includes delivery, implementation, maintenance, warranty, and tax.
- Please clarify any assumptions, exclusions, or dependencies.

## IC Summary Drafting

Use this structure:

Background:
Summarize business need, scope, timeline, and procurement route.

Commercial Comparison:
Summarize vendors, quoted prices, variance, key assumptions, and negotiation result.

Key Risks:
Summarize operational, commercial, compliance, transition, and supplier risks.

Recommendation:
Provide a recommendation for management consideration, not final approval.

Decision Required:
State what decision or endorsement is requested.
